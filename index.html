The International Workshop on Federated Learning Technologies and Emerging Systems (FLTES 2027)

FLTES 2027 is organized with the International Conference on Next Generation Cyber Security (NGCS 2027), taking place on March 15–18, 2027 in San Antonio, Texas, USA.

The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners building federated and decentralized learning systems, with a particular focus on the privacy, security, robustness, and deployment challenges of training models across many parties without centralizing their data.

Call for Papers

Submit original work on secure, private, and scalable federated learning for emerging systems.

FLTES 2027 invites researchers, practitioners, and industry experts to submit original contributions on federated learning and its role in emerging systems. As learning moves to hospitals, vehicles, phones, factories, and critical infrastructure, data increasingly stays where it is produced. Federated learning makes collaboration possible under these constraints, but it also introduces new attack surfaces, privacy risks, heterogeneity problems, and operational challenges.

The workshop welcomes theoretical, empirical, experimental, and applied work, including full research papers, short papers, position papers, case studies, tool demonstrations, datasets, benchmarks, and lessons learned from real-world deployments.

Scope. FLTES 2027 focuses on two closely connected research directions.

Direction 01

Trustworthy federated learning

The first is trustworthy federated learning, covering methods that protect federated systems against poisoning, backdoors, free-riding, inference attacks, and gradient leakage, together with privacy-enhancing techniques such as differential privacy, secure aggregation, homomorphic encryption, and trusted execution environments.

Topics of interest

  • Poisoning, backdoor, and Byzantine-robust federated learning
  • Gradient leakage, membership inference, and privacy attacks on FL
  • Secure aggregation, homomorphic encryption, and MPC for FL
  • Differential privacy and privacy accounting in federated settings
  • Federated learning with trusted execution environments
  • Heterogeneity, non-IID data, personalization, and fairness in FL
  • Incentives, auditing, unlearning, and governance of federations
Direction 02

Federated learning for emerging systems

The second direction is federated learning for emerging systems, focusing on how federated, split, and decentralized learning can be engineered and deployed at scale across edge, IoT, 6G, vehicular, healthcare, industrial, and cyber-physical environments, including federated foundation models, federated LLM fine-tuning, and federated approaches to cyber threat detection.

Topics of interest

  • Communication-efficient, asynchronous, and hierarchical FL
  • Federated fine-tuning of foundation models and LLMs
  • Decentralized, peer-to-peer, split, and blockchain-based learning
  • Federated learning for intrusion detection and threat intelligence
  • FL for edge, IoT, 6G, vehicular, and cyber-physical systems
  • Federated learning in healthcare, finance, and industrial settings
  • Frameworks, benchmarks, testbeds, and reproducibility for FL
